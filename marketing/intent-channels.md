# Selling to people already looking (intent-based outreach)

Cold teardowns interrupt someone who wasn't shopping. This file is the opposite: places
where someone has **already decided to pay a developer** and is actively looking for one.
Higher close rate, shorter cycle, no need to convince anyone they have a problem.

> **The asset gap.** Every marketing asset you have is built for interruption — the audit
> tool, the teardown PDF, the "I noticed X on your site" opener. None of that fits a channel
> where the buyer is already in-market. There, you're being **compared**, not discovered, and
> what wins is a fast, specific, credible reply. Template at the bottom.

---

## Tier 1 — explicit hiring intent (work these first)

### 1. Contra — free, no bidding fees
`contra.com` · Free to apply, no paid credits. The lowest-friction place to start today.
Set up the profile once, then apply daily.

### 2. Upwork — highest volume of real budgets
`upwork.com/nx/find-work/` · Filter: **Fixed price**, **$500+**, **Posted: Last 24 hours**.
⚠️ Applying costs **Connects** (paid, with a small free monthly allowance). Given the zero-
budget constraint, spend them only where you're a genuine fit — 3 good proposals beat 15
sprayed ones, and Upwork's algorithm rewards a high reply rate anyway.

### 3. Facebook groups — free, high volume, underrated
Join and set to "New posts". People post "looking for a web developer" daily:
- Shopify Entrepreneurs / Shopify Store Owners
- WordPress / WooCommerce Help
- Local: PH business owner groups, plus AU/NZ small-business groups (your existing client base)

### 4. LinkedIn — free, search-based
Search these, then filter **Posts** → **Past 24 hours**:
- `"looking for a web developer"`
- `"need a website built"`
- `"anyone know a good Shopify developer"`
- `"hiring a freelance developer"`

Comment publicly *and* DM. Your profile already links to the studio site.

### 5. Reddit — free, explicit
`r/forhire` and `r/jobbit`, sort **New**, look for `[HIRING]`. Also `r/shopify`,
`r/smallbusiness`, `r/Entrepreneur` for "can someone help me with…" posts.
(These need a logged-in browser — Reddit blocks scripted access now.)

## Tier 2 — problem-in-public (slower, compounds)

### Shopify Community help boards
`community.shopify.com` — people posting today with things they can't fix. Verified live
this morning; real examples from the last 24 hours:
- "How to Show Main Product Image First Instead of Variant Image" (3 replies)
- "Limit 1 order per customer" (6 replies)
- "Store slower after uninstalling apps"

**This is a reputation channel, not a hiring channel.** Answer genuinely and completely,
for free, with no pitch. The paid work comes from the DM afterwards, or from the next
person who searches the same problem and finds your answer. Budget 15 min/day, expect
results in weeks not days.

⚠️ Some "help" posts are app vendors marketing themselves (UprightApps appeared twice in
today's feed). Check the poster before investing a long answer.

## The proposal template (for Tier 1)

On a job post you're one of 20+ replies and the client skims for ~10 seconds. Rules:

1. **No preamble.** Never "I am a passionate developer with 6+ years." Delete the first
   paragraph you write — it's always throat-clearing.
2. **Prove you read the post** in line one, with a specific detail from it.
3. **Show, don't claim.** Link the closest of your 17 live builds — same industry or same
   platform. That's the differentiator nobody else in the thread has.
4. **One question.** It signals you're thinking about the job, and it starts a conversation
   instead of ending on a price.
5. **Short.** Six lines. Anything longer gets skimmed past.

> Hi — you mentioned [specific detail from their post, e.g. "migrating off Wix without
> losing your existing product URLs"]. That redirect mapping is the part that usually goes
> wrong, and it's the first thing I'd lock down.
>
> I've done this on [X] stores — closest to yours is [multihullcentral.com] (Shopify,
> custom theme). It's live, you can click through it.
>
> Fixed price, fixed timeline, and you own the code and the accounts at the end.
>
> One question: is [the specific unknown — e.g. "the current site on Wix's own checkout or
> an embedded one?"] — that changes the approach quite a bit.
>
> — Raymund, Forgeline Technologies · forgelinetechnologies.com

**Optional differentiator:** run `site-audit` on their current site (30 seconds) and add one
true observation. That turns a generic proposal into "I already looked." Use the tool as a
*research* step here, not as the offer.
